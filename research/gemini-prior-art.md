### Answers to Your Three Questions

#### (1) Is there prior work that already learns an animal's behaviour-selection policy (when each behaviour happens) from video of specific individuals?

**Partially in computational ethology and multi-agent trajectory forecasting, yes; for interactive physics-driven game characters with internal drives, no.**

* **What exists:** 
  * In computational neuroscience/ethology, works building on **MoSeq / VAME / B-SOiD** and **CalMS21** (Sun et al., 2021—social mouse interaction benchmarks) learn individual-specific transition matrices or autoregressive policies predicting $P(\text{regime}_{t+1} \mid \text{sensory\_context}_t, \text{partner\_pose}_t)$. Specifically, **Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) / Markov Decision Process modeling of animal behavior** (e.g., Ashwood et al., *ICLR/NeurIPS* 2022, "Dynamic routing / GLM-HMMs" for animal decision-making) infers latent internal states ("engaged", "biased/tired", "disengaged") that govern *when* a specific individual obeys a sensory cue versus ignoring it.
  * In graphics/robotics, **generative behavior/traffic/sports models** learn individual playstyles from video, and **Hierarchical RL works** (like Han et al. 2024 or ASE/CALM) learn high-level routers—*but* those routers are trained via **task rewards in simulation**, not cloned from longitudinal home video of specific pets.
* **What is missing (your novel/hard gap):** Home video only shows *observational* data: observable context $\rightarrow$ chosen regime. It does **not** record the dog's unobservable internal drives (hunger, fatigue, arousal) and exhibits heavy **confounding / covariate shift** (e.g., in home videos, you probably stop asking the Labrador to fetch once it lies down panting, so the dataset lacks counterfactuals of "player spams fetch command 20 times while dog is exhausted"). Pure behavioral cloning of your video sequences will suffer from causal confusion unless you explicitly structure latent state inference (like GLM-HMMs) or hybridize the learned transition priors with designer-authored drive decay curves.

#### (2) What is the most proven path for the low-level motor layer for a dog on a single RTX 3090?

Do **not** try to train low-level motor skills from your 2D home videos, and do **not** start with unsupervised adversarial skill discovery (ASE) from scratch. On a single RTX 3090 (24GB VRAM), the most proven, low-headache path in 2025/2026 is a **Track-then-Distill (PHC $\rightarrow$ PULSE / MaskedMimic style) or CALM pipeline in Isaac Lab or MuJoCo Playground**:

1. **Simulator Choice:** Use **Isaac Lab** (PhysX 5 GPU) or **MuJoCo Playground (MJX)**. On a single 3090, running 4,096 parallel dog environments takes ~6–10 GB of VRAM and trains a basic locomotion/tracking policy in **15–45 minutes**, and a full multi-skill repertoire in **8–20 hours**.
2. **Data Bootstrapping (Don't use home video here yet):** Start with the public **Zhang et al. (2018) 3D dog mocap dataset** (has walk, trot, pace, canter, jump, sit, lie down, stand, sniff). Retarget it twice: once to a custom Labrador rigid-body rig (heavier torso, thicker tail) and once to a Malinois rig (lighter waist, higher torque-to-weight ratio). For missing skills ("shake off"), keyframe 2 seconds in Blender or extract 3D poses from 3–4 clean multi-angle/tripod clips using **BITE** (3D dog pose estimator) + trajectory optimization. Do *not* need mocap for "fall and recover"—RL invents that when initialized in random fallen states.
3. **Stage A – Universal Motion Tracker (PHC-style):** Train a single PPO goal-conditioned tracking policy that imitates all retargeted clips + recovers from random pushes and fallen ragdoll initializations. Tracking rewards (joint positions, rotations, velocities, foot contacts) **never suffer from GAN mode collapse**, so rare skills like *lie down* and *shake off* are guaranteed to survive.
4. **Stage B – Controllable Distillation (PULSE / CALM / MaskedMimic-lite):** Distill or wrap that tracker into a latent-conditioned policy (or train discrete/continuous skill heads with an AMP style-discriminator regularizer for smooth transitions) that accepts `(regime_id, target_root_velocity, target_head_lookat_point)`. This gives your high-level router clean, named handles (`SIT`, `TROT`, `LIE_DOWN`, `SHAKE`) while physics seamlessly handles transitions, uneven ground, and dog-on-dog bumps.

#### (3) What is the riskiest part of our design and the cheapest experiment that would expose it?

**The Riskiest Part:** **Observational confounding and missing context in the home-video-learned "Router" (specifically, causal confusion when transitioning from passive video prediction to interactive closed-loop control).**

In home video:
1. Many triggers for a dog changing regimes are **off-camera or non-visual** (a squirrel outside the window, a subtle human kissy-noise, how long the walk was *before* filming started, floor slipperiness).
2. Because DLC keypoints are 2D camera-space (unless you do full 3D scene reconstruction), "handler walking toward dog" vs. "camera zooming in" gets conflated.
3. When a VLM labels clips and you train a sequence model on `(senses, drives, handler) -> regime`, the model will cheat via **autoregressive inertia** (predicting `LIE_DOWN` at $t+1$ simply because it was `LIE_DOWN` at $t$, ignoring handler inputs) OR fail out-of-distribution when a player behaves differently than the real owner in the videos ( creating deadlocks where the dog refuses *everything* or flickers rapidly between regimes every 0.5 seconds).

**The Cheapest Experiment to Expose It (1–2 Weeks, Zero Physics/3D Required):**

Build a **"2D Top-Down Dot-Dog Prototype"** before writing a single line of MuJoCo/Isaac code:
1. Take **2 hours of existing home video** (1 hr Lab, 1 hr Malinois) covering mixed interactions (fetch, lounging, ignoring commands, two-dog play).
2. Run your exact proposed perception pipeline: **DeepLabCut** (or SLEAP) + a quick homography/depth heuristic (e.g., DepthAnythingV2) to project the two dogs and handler onto an approximate **2D top-down metric floorplane** (positions, headings, relative distances, velocities).
3. Run your **VLM** (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro / GPT-4o / Qwen2.5-VL) on 2-second sliding windows to extract `(dog_regime, handler_command_if_any)` and manually audit 200 clips to measure VLM label noise (VLMs frequently confuse *play bow*, *stretch*, and *lying down*, or miss subtle hand/voice cues).
4. Train your proposed regime classifier + sequence router, hook it up to **three circles on a 2D PyGame canvas** (Player circle controlled by WASD + buttons for `Call`, `Sit`, `Throw Ball`; Lab circle; Malinois circle, where regimes just print text labels above the circles and move them at heuristic speeds), and **play with it for 15 minutes**.

**Pass/Fail Criteria:** Within 15 minutes of interactive 2D play, you will immediately see whether the two dots *feel* distinctly like your specific Lab and Malinois (e.g., Malinois obsessively shadowing the ball/handler while the Lab wanders off to sniff and declines `Sit` until approached), **or** whether causal confusion makes them catatonic, hyper-flickery, or unresponsive to player actions. Exposing that in PyGame costs days; discovering it after 4 months of RL physics rig tuning would be fatal.
