# The whole dog: what has to exist

The goal is a game dog, two of them: your Labrador and your Malinois. People who know them should accept each as alive and as that particular dog. Each takes requests and keeps the final say, does what real dogs do (retrieve, track, play, rest), and runs on a gaming PC.

This file maps every part that has to exist. It marks which parts are already solved somewhere, which are open, and what your footage supplies. Requirements and their status: [REQUIREMENTS.md](REQUIREMENTS.md).

## What is solved and what isn't
Almost every layer is a solved problem somewhere: tracking, rigs, motion matching, physics-based control, utility AI, scent and wind, even final say. What has never been done is putting them together as two particular dogs, under the rules in your first notes. Two things are open:
- **3D from casual video.** Getting your dogs' movement out of handheld phone footage and into 3D. This is the biggest technical risk.
- **Assembly.** One coherent dog in which these layers drive each other. That is the work.

## First slice
The first slice is one embodied, verifiable piece, chosen as the cheapest one that tests the most layers:
- **A dog at rest who answers a request.** It lies breathing, shifts, flicks its ears and looks around. Asked to come, it decides whether to, rises, stretches, may shake, walks over, and lies back down.
- **Layers it tests:** body (4), regimes and transitions (5), physiology (7), decision with final say (9) and individuality at rest (10).
- **What it needs from the footage:** static resting clips, which exist (IMG_7021, IMG_4315, IMG_5028, IMG_1352).
- **What it leaves out:** water, two-dog contact and hunting.
- **Why rest first:** the opposition argued that life shows in the stillness and the in-between, and this slice tests that cheaply with people.

Second slice: the retrieve, on land, then water. The Lab's 13 creek retrieves hold its phases and timing. After that: the two dogs together, which is most of what they do on camera.
