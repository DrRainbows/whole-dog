# Shot list: footage that decides between the design and its rivals

Compiled from the deciding tests in [README.md](README.md) and the rival build specs (available on request). Every row is a recording of the two real dogs. None needs anything built first.

**How to film.** Phone, side view, 60 fps or the slow-motion mode where noted. Keep the sound. Keep the seconds before and after. Keep the failures and the awkward bits; they are the data.

**Do not perturb the dogs.** Two of the models' tests pull a rug from under a sitting dog or shove it sideways. Don't. Rows 7 and 8 get the same information from ground the dogs already walk on.

| # | film | how | what it decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Twenty retrieves in a row | Same spot, same throw, fixed interval. Rest ten minutes, then five more | Does motion cost something? Timing and amplitude should decay, then recover |
| 2 | The same recall eight times in ninety seconds | Same place, same posture, same tone | Is there a visible price for repeating? Look at the spread, not the average |
| 3 | A hand-offer that freezes halfway | Start to offer, stop for a second and a half, then either resume or change. Twenty times over several days | Does the dog finish the shape, or slow, check your face and repair? |
| 4 | Food it has to fit its body to reach | Under a low shelf, behind a chair leg, under a blanket, through a narrow gap. Thirty tries per dog | Is behavior "close on the target", or the body finding a fit? |
| 5 | Five minutes of nothing | Dog alone in a room, no requests, camera on a shelf. Several sessions | Is there life in the gaps? The pauses are also the clips for the gaps-only test below |
| 6 | A target swap | Show a toy, take it away, show a better one somewhere else | Does switching take time and leave a blend? Measure from removal to the head pointing at the new one |
| 7 | Ground that tests the feet | Turns on a smooth floor, wet grass, a slope, stairs, a jump down. Slow motion | How it catches itself. Count foot replants and trunk counter-turns |
| 8 | Things that trail | A fast head turn at rest, a shake-off, a landing. Slow motion, close | Do ears, jowls, skin and tail lag and overshoot after the skull stops? |
| 9 | Heel on a figure-eight | Walk the eight at two speeds, with stops | Is heel a held distance, or bearing and speed matching through the turns? |
| 10 | Sound only | Phone close to the dog while it walks on a hard floor, pants, drinks, settles, shakes | Can people who know the dog pick it out by sound alone? |
| 11 | Three tempos | Stand with the dog and clap or step slowly, then fast, then in between. Thirty seconds each | Does its wag or weight shift lock to your tempo? |
| 12 | Something it can't see | A sound from another room; a treat hidden while it's out | Search and orienting with no visible target |
| 13 | How it rests | Whatever lying and sitting shapes turn up over a week, uncued | One foreleg tucked and one out, a paw on a knee: shapes the posture grid can't hold |

## Tests on the footage, before anything is built

Show people who know the two dogs a clip with everything removed except one carrier, and ask which dog it is.

| carrier left in | how to make it | if people can still tell the dogs apart |
|---|---|---|
| Movement only | Blacked-out silhouette, or dots at the joints from pose tracking | Identity lives in weight and timing |
| Sound only | Row 10 | Sound carries more than the design assumes |
| Pauses only | Row 5, cut to the stretches where nothing nameable happens | Identity lives between the names |
| Names only | A time-stamped list of lexicon entries for the clip, no video | The design's bet: a dog is thresholds over a shared list |

The last row needs more than two dogs, or breed gives it away. Borrow clips of two more Labradors and two more Malinois.

Whichever carrier holds identity is what to build first.
